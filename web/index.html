Kidney swaps
you can check.

Some patients have a willing donor who doesn't match them. When a few of these pairs swap donors, everyone gets a kidney that fits. Swap finds the best swaps, then shows exactly why, for every patient, matched or not.

Go to the results

What makes Swap different

  1. It explains the "no".

    Pick any patient who missed out. Swap shows which donors could have helped, and what got in the way.

  2. It never guesses.

    Every answer carries its evidence. If the graph has nothing, it says the graph doesn't know, never "safe". The AI helper can only choose from fixed, human-written queries.

  3. It proposes. People decide.

    Swap drafts the plan and records it in the graph. Only a person can approve it, and the AI helper has no way to.

  4. It checks itself.

    Every number is recounted by separate code that never touches the database. If they ever disagree, you see it.

Why a graph? A swap is a loop of "donor can give to patient" links. Finding loops, and asking "who could give to this patient?", are two things a does naturally, from the same stored links.

          Honest limits

          • Synthetic data only. Every patient is generated from a seed; nothing here is a real person.
          • A known result, reproduced. The 2-way vs 3-way effect is the finding of Roth, Sönmez and Ünver (2007). We re-implement it; we do not discover it.
          • The upper bound is not a plan. The max-flow figure also allows chains, so it is not a set of swaps you could perform.
          • Not medical advice. Decision support for a coordinator, on synthetic data, with no clinical validation.
