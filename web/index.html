Finding kidneys by swapping donors

Some patients have a willing kidney donor who is the wrong match. Swap finds chains of donor swaps so more people get a kidney, and shows exactly why.

  • Every answer walks a real graph database (FalkorDB)
  • Every number is checked against an independent count
  • You never have to read a query to use it

How it works

Three ideas are enough to follow the whole page.

  1. 1

    A pair, but no match

    A patient (left) has a willing donor (right). Often the blood type or the patient's immune response (sensitisation) means the donor cannot give to their own patient.

  2. 2

    Swap donors, not kidneys

    If two pairs swap donors, both patients get one (2-way). Add a third pair and they can swap in a ring (3-way). More pairs allowed, more patients matched.

  3. 3

    The graph finds every swap

    The graph database (FalkorDB) finds every possible swap, then a solver picks the best set that shares no patient. The highlighted ring is one such choice.

Honest limits

  • Synthetic data only. Every patient is generated from a seed; nothing here is a real person.
  • A known result, reproduced. The 2-way vs 3-way effect is the finding of Roth, Sönmez and Ünver (2007). We re-implement it; we do not discover it.
  • Part of the work happens outside the database. FalkorDB finds the candidate swaps, the bound and the explanations; a separate integer-program solver picks the best set.
  • The upper bound is not a plan. The max-flow figure also allows chains, so it is not a set of swaps you could perform.
  • Not medical advice. Decision support for a coordinator, on synthetic data, with no clinical validation.
